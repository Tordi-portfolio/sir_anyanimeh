Introduction

Carbon dioxide plays an important role in petroleum engineering, particularly in enhanced oil recovery (EOR), carbon capture and storage (CCS), reservoir simulation, and pipeline transportation.

Because CO₂ behaves as a real gas, the ideal gas law cannot accurately describe its behavior under reservoir conditions. Therefore, a correction factor known as the compressibility factor (Z-factor) is introduced.

02

The Compressibility Factor (Z-Factor)

The compressibility factor is a dimensionless quantity that measures the deviation of a real gas from ideal-gas behavior.

Z = PV / nRT

Where:

SymbolDescription
ZCompressibility factor
PPressure
VGas volume
nNumber of moles
RUniversal gas constant
TAbsolute temperature
03

Polynomial Correlation

This project uses a third-order polynomial correlation to estimate the compressibility factor.

Z = a + bP + cT + dP² + eT² + fPT + gP³ + hT³ + iP²T + jPT²

The coefficients used in this study are fixed.

04

Methodology

The methodology used in this project consists of four stages.

  1. The user enters pressure and temperature values.
  2. The polynomial correlation calculates the Z-factor.
  3. The application generates a results table.
  4. A graph of Z-factor versus pressure is plotted for different temperatures.
05

Graph Interpretation

The graph generated by the application uses:

  • X-axis = Pressure (psi)
  • Y-axis = CO₂ Z-factor
  • Curves = Temperature (°C)

A decrease in the Z-factor indicates a greater deviation from ideal-gas behavior. An increase in the Z-factor indicates that the gas is becoming less compressible.
